AI+RES · Phase 1 · Gate 3

Can a cheap forecast tell you which walker is going to be extreme?

Gates 1 and 2 showed the GenCast→FourCastNet 3 handoff is faithful. Neither showed the resulting score is useful. This one does — and it shows the free baseline it has to beat is worth nothing at the leads that matter.

GATE 3: PASS ρs ≥ 0.5 required at tk = 9 d and 12 d
The FourCastNet 3 score reaches ρs = 0.797 at 9 days (p = 2×10−4) and 0.959 at 12 days (p < 10−4). Both bootstrap confidence intervals exclude the threshold. Phase 1 is complete; the Diffusion Monte Carlo loop is cleared to be built.

What the gate measures

Sixteen GenCast walkers run free from the event initialization to the peak, writing a global restart state every three days. Each checkpoint is converted into a FourCastNet 3 initial condition and scored by six FCN3 members run forward to the peak: 

walker state at t_k  →  adapter  →  6 FCN3 forecasts to the peak
                     →  A_L per member  →  theta = mean

The gate is the Spearman correlation, across walkers, between theta(t_k) and each walker’s own realized A_L. Note what the ground truth is: not ERA5, but the walker’s outcome. The score does not have to be right about the atmosphere, only about the walker — that is all resampling reads, and it is the only thing a score function can fairly be held to.

The observable is the 7-day mean 2 m temperature anomaly over an event-centered box (44–50°N, 124–118°W). That choice is not cosmetic: the observed anomaly is +7.72 K over the box and +0.64 K over CONUS.

The persistence column is the substantive result. Lancelin et al.’s Standard-RES scores a walker by the observable’s current value — free, no emulator. At both gate leads that baseline has no skill at all (0.062 and 0.129, inside the noise) while FCN3 reaches 0.80 and 0.96. Whatever FCN3 is doing, it is not reading off the current state. That is the entire justification for the GPU hours a production run will spend.

At N = 16 walkers, ρs > 0.426 is the 5% one-sided significance level, so the 0.5 threshold means “significantly better than chance” and not much more. The 9 d and 12 d results clear it by far more than that; the 6 d result sits close to it and should be read as suggestive.

The four panels

Two findings that constrain the phases after this

C_k SCHEDULE 3 → 6 d where skill switches on

At tk = 3 d the score is worthless (ρs = −0.044): FCN3 is being asked for an 18-day forecast. It crosses 0.5 at about 5.5 d. The plan’s C_1 = 0 is therefore right and the schedule must not move earlier — C_2 = 0 is worth testing too.

ENSEMBLE SIZE M = 6 84% / 97% of ceiling

The largest correlation θ could reach with any target, given its own M = 6 sampling error, is 0.952 at 9 d and 0.990 at 12 d. FCN3 already runs at 84% and 97% of that. Raising M buys almost nothing where resampling actually happens.

Sixteen direct samples get to +7.49 K and stop just short of the observed +7.72 K. That is precisely the question the pilot run is set up to answer: does resampling reach a tail that direct sampling misses?

The second row settles the box question empirically. On the CONUS mean this “extreme” is unremarkable — 13 of 16 walkers already exceed it — so a CONUS-mean experiment would have had no tail to find.

Provenance: two runs were thrown away first

The walkers were rolling the wrong model. gencast_s2s.config.model_cfg takes a res argument but uses it only for the ERA5 coarsening stride — the checkpoint is params_file or GENCAST_PARAMS, and that default is the 1.0° Mini model. So load_gencast(res=0.25) silently loaded Mini and ran it on 0.25° fields. Nothing raised: GraphCast builds its mesh from whatever grid the inputs carry.

The output was not subtle once looked at. CONUS-mean T2m lost its diurnal cycle inside the first three-day segment — 00Z equal to 12Z to 0.03 K — drifted 26 K cold over 21 days, and realized AL came out at −22 K against an observed +7.7 K. It was caught because that number is impossible, not because any check fired.

The first fix patched one of the two model loaders and missed the one the walk phase actually runs, so a second 8-GPU allocation went the same way. The duplication was the real defect.

A controlled comparison on one GPU then established that the rollout itself was never at fault. Against the cached reference ensemble’s member 0, CONUS-mean T2m over six steps: 

                     step1   step2   step3   step4   step5   step6
reference member 0  292.424 298.672 292.751 298.554 292.386 298.733
xres 21-day batch   292.422 298.672 292.753 298.550 292.382 298.744
walker 6-step batch 292.420 298.673 292.750 298.554 292.373 298.724

Agreement to ~0.01 K, including with the exact per-segment RNG key the walk pool uses. The walker’s batch construction, forcings, rolling buffer, restart handoff and cloning RNG are all correct. Only the checkpoint was ever wrong.

Three things now prevent a recurrence: a single loader that reads the same resolution registry the reference cubes were made with; a physical check after the first segment comparing CONUS-mean T2m against that reference ensemble (298.56 ± 0.162 K at 3 d — the broken walkers were 71 standard deviations out, the correct ones came in at 0.2–3.8); and every state now recording the checkpoint that produced it.
