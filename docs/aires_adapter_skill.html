Does the adapter cost forecast skill?

Gate 2 showed that FCN3 rolled from an adapter-built initial condition lands in the same place as FCN3 rolled from ERA5 directly. It never asked whether either forecast was any good. Scoring both 24-member ensembles against observed truth: the adapter changes skill by less than the ensemble's own sampling noise - and neither ensemble forecasts the heat dome.

The answer, in three numbers

The effect is smaller than the noise it would have to beat

Both ensembles start from the same ERA5 analysis at the same time. They differ only in how the 72 input channels were assembled: natively from ERA5, or through aires/adapter.py. Each is verified against the observed 7-day-mean T2m anomaly over the week ending at the event peak - the quantity AI+RES scores walkers on.

Is the difference real?

Every difference lands inside one ensemble's own scatter

A 24-member ensemble mean is itself a random variable. To size that, the native ensemble was split into two disjoint halves 2000 times and each half scored separately: the gap between two halves of the same forecast is what "no difference" looks like. Which way does it lean?

Probably slightly worse - by an amount this experiment cannot measure

"Inside the noise" is not the same as "zero", so it is worth saying which way the point estimates point. On most ensemble-mean and probabilistic scores, under both truth datasets, the adapter run is the marginally worse of the two.

The tight version of the test resamples the 24 matched member pairs with replacement - preserving the seed pairing, which the split-half floor above throws away - and rescores both ensembles on each draw:

Three reasons not to call it a measured degradation

  • The two tightest tests disagree in sign. Seed-matched per-member RMSE over CONUS makes the adapter marginally better (−0.050 ± 0.154 K), as does its CONUS AL bias - the adapter mean sits 0.21 K from the observed +0.64 K where native sits 0.29 K away. Meanwhile the ensemble-mean RMSE over the same region makes it worse. When the sign depends on which noise-dominated reduction you pick, the effect is not resolved.
  • The comparisons are not independent. Every row above comes from the same 24 member pairs on the same event at the same init. A consistent lean across twelve correlated statistics carries roughly one event's worth of evidence, not twelve.
  • Direction was never a coin flip, so consistency is weak evidence. The adapter IC carries real added error against the native one - Gate 1 measured 0.63 m/s on u100m/v100m and 4.6% on tcwv - and adding error to an initial condition cannot improve a forecast in expectation. A tiny degradation is the prior, not a discovery. The data is consistent with it and also consistent with zero.

The defensible statement

The adapter very likely costs a little skill, and this experiment bounds that cost rather than measuring it: below ~0.3 K of CONUS RMSE and ~1.4 K over the event box at 21-day lead - under the ensemble's own sampling noise, and far under the 0.83 K by which ERA5 and HRRR disagree about what happened. For AI+RES that bound is what matters, because the walker score is an ensemble mean and a bias smaller than the sampling noise cannot reorder walkers.

To actually measure it would take more events, not more members. Five other week-3 events already have native FCN3 cubes on disk (WinterStorm_Uri_2021, HurricaneIan_2022, and three p90_* cases); building their adapter counterparts is CPU prep plus roughly an hour of one 8×H100 node - and it turns a lean across correlated statistics into a sign test across six independent events.

Why a null result was the only possible outcome

By day 21 the adapter is arithmetically a re-seed

Two divergence curves can be compared directly, because the two ensembles share noise streams member for member. dpaired is the RMS adapter-minus-native difference - the adapter's footprint. dinternal is √2 × the native ensemble's own spread - what re-seeding FCN3 does on its own. The adapter's footprint starts at 22% of the model's own stochasticity and reaches 99% of it only at day 21, exactly where this verification lives.

That is the mechanism behind the null: at the verification window an adapter IC and a re-seed are the same size of perturbation, so a skill difference between them cannot be anything but sampling noise. The interesting part of the curve is the left half, where the adapter is a genuinely smaller perturbation than the model's own noise.

The finding that is not about the adapter

Neither ensemble forecasts the heat dome

The adapter question is answered, and it is the less interesting half of this page. Over the PNW box the observed 7-day-mean anomaly was +7.72 K. The ensemble means are +1.52 K (adapter) and +2.29 K (native) - a fifth and under a third of the event respectively. Exactly one member out of 24 on each side reaches the observed value.

Nor is the ensemble honest about that miss. Spread ÷ error over the box is 0.46 and 0.60: the ensemble is confidently wrong, not widely uncertain. GenCast 0.25° at the same lead behaves the same way (0.53, zero members reaching the observed value), so this is a property of 21-day-lead forecasting rather than of FCN3 or of the adapter.

Read the two results in the right order

"The adapter is free" is a statement about the plumbing - the score a walker receives is a property of the walker, not of how its state was converted. That is what Gate 2 needed and it holds.

"The forecast misses the dome" is a statement about the atmosphere at 21-day lead. It is not a defect the adapter introduced, and it does not weaken the gate. It is the premise of the experiment.

What this test cannot say

The resolution of the null

"No detected cost" is bounded by what 24 members on one event can detect. Stated plainly, rather than left for a reader to infer:

  • Detection limit. At two paired standard errors, this test would only catch an RMSE degradation larger than ±0.31 K over CONUS (19% of the native run's 1.64 K) or ±1.41 K over the PNW box (25% of 5.52 K). A smaller real penalty would pass unseen.
  • One event, one init. Everything here is the 2021 PNW heat dome from 2021-06-07. The adapter's three derived channels (u100m, v100m, tcwv) come from a calibration fitted on 90 held-out ICs, so the Gate 1 error bars generalize; this skill comparison does not.
  • The truth itself is uncertain by more than the effect. ERA5 puts the observed PNW anomaly at +7.72 K; the HRRR analysis puts it at +6.89 K. That 0.83 K disagreement about what happened exceeds every adapter-minus-native difference on this page. Repeating the scoring against HRRR moves both runs together and leaves the gap between them essentially unchanged (CONUS 0.05 K, PNW 0.73 K).
  • One lead, one variable. Skill is verified only at the 21-day verification window, on T2m, because that is the only truth field cached for this event. A lead-resolved skill curve would need an ERA5 time series that is not on disk here.
