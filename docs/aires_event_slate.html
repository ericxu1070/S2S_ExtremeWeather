GenCast walkers · FourCastNet 3 score · 21-day lead · N = 64

AI+RES Event Slate

Nine CONUS cases put through rare-event resampling at week-3 lead, from a heat dome no direct ensemble reaches to an ordinary Friday in August. Each one gets its observed anomaly, how rare that was against 64 years of ERA5, and the probability the run assigns to it.

Every number on this page reduces to one observable:

AL = cos(lat)-weighted mean, over the event box, of the 7-day-mean 2 m temperature anomaly ending at the event peak

GenCast is initialised 21 days before that peak and rolled as 64 walkers. At five barriers FourCastNet 3 scores each walker's expected AL, the promising ones are cloned and the rest killed, and a weight wi undoes the tilt so the surviving population still estimates GenCast's own probabilities. The C schedule (0, 1.0, 1.4, 1.8, 2.0) was tuned once, on the Pacific Northwest, and has not been re-tuned for any event since.

Two different questions live in the two percentile columns and they answer differently on purpose. The climatological percentile is how rare the weather was: the same index computed from 64 years of ERA5, pooled within ±21 days of the event's calendar position. σ-depth is how hard it was to forecast: where the observation sat in the 24-member GenCast ensemble launched three weeks earlier. The South-Central heat dome is the rarest case here and only the fifth-hardest to reach.

The whole population

all ten runs cross-event

Every number in the table above is a sum over the walkers of one run. This is that raw material, one panel per run, in the same hardest-first order: where each of the 64 surviving walkers landed on AL, and the probability mass pi = Z·wi/N it carries. The shaded band is the mass that sums to the headline P.

Three things are legible here that a table cannot show. Reach is bought and then charged for: the blue line is 1/N, what a walker would be worth unresampled, and the deepest walkers sit one to two decades below it - the Pacific Northwest's hottest carries 2.5 % of what a single direct member is worth. The masses come in tied blocks: walkers cloned at the last barrier inherit their parent's mass exactly, so Uri's 64 walkers hold 34 distinct values and the persistence control's hold 15, and that count - not 64 - is the number of independent pieces of information behind the estimate. Two runs cannot answer their own question: Southwest and the control have nothing in the band, so P is 0 and the honest statement is the deepest level the run resolved (3.2 and 2.6 × 10−4); 7 November 2023 has the opposite failure, its observation below every walker, so the band covers the whole panel and P is just the run's own normalization check.

The trajectory panels below now also carry the operational CFSv2 forecast at the same 21-day lead, on all nine events rather than the original four. It reaches the observation only on the two mildest rungs - 3 of 4 members on 7 November 2023, 2 of 4 on 2 August 2024 - and misses every genuinely extreme one, including the 24 December 2025 warm spell by 4.6 K with its ensemble mean on the wrong side of climatology. That is not a resolution artefact: pushing the ERA5 truth field through CFS's own ~0.94° grid and re-scoring it moves AL by at most 0.13 K, against gaps of 2.3 to 11.6 K.

The cause is structural and was checked numerically rather than inferred. Resampling tilted the whole population warm, so its lowest realized A_L is +1.498 K - above the +0.211 K target - and the indicator 1{A_L ≥ observed} is identically 1 across all 64 walkers. The estimate Z·mean(w·1{...}) then collapses to Z·mean(w), which is exactly the normalization check: 0.582662 against 0.582662, to every digit printed. The estimator cannot return a probability above its own normalization check, and the truth sits above that ceiling. Self-normalizing is no better - it returns exactly 1.000, because “every walker reached it” is all the information the population retains. Nothing inside the run reveals any of this: a reader of compare.json alone would take 0.583 at face value. The rule it establishes is that AI+RES has no resolution at a target the tilt has already carried the entire population past, so the usable operating range is targets the resampled population still straddles - which the run one rung up does (its population minimum is +0.39 K against a +1.06 K target) and this one does not.

The persistence control

same walkers, same schedule, different score attribution not reached

Every gain above is attributed to the FCN3 score by this one run. It repeats the Pacific Northwest pilot with the same walkers, the same resampling, the same C schedule and the same seeds, swapping only the function that decides which walkers to clone - from a skilful forecast of the event to “who is hottest now”. It reaches the observed +7.72 K with 0 of 64 walkers.

The selection is noise at this lead: ESS/N falls to 0.04 and 0.02 at steps 3 and 4 with clone multiplicities of 39 and 49, three of 64 founders survive, the weights span 0.013 to 869, and the normalization check comes out at 8.75 with a weighted mean of +0.29 K - the weights cancel the tilt entirely. Cloning on a skilful forecast of the event beats cloning on the present state, which is exactly what the Gate 3 persistence column predicted before any of these runs were made.

Where the method's limits now sit

Two boundaries are measured rather than assumed, and both were found by runs that did not go the way the plan expected. The upper limit is between +2.8 and +3.3 σ: Winter Storm Elliott at +2.81 σ is reached and the Southwest heat wave at +3.3 σ is not, and because one is a freeze and one a heat wave the boundary is not sign-dependent. The lower limit is a target the tilt has already carried the whole population past, where the estimate pins to the run's own normalization check and reports a probability that is silently meaningless. The method's usable range is the span between them: targets the resampled population still straddles, and that direct sampling still misses.

Provenance

Climatology from python -m aires.aclim --report, run on Derecho on 26 August 2026 against a freshly built cache: WeatherBench-2 daily-mean 0.25° ERA5 for 1959-2023, topped up from ARCO for the post-2023 peaks, anomalies against the same 1990-2019 baseline the experiment uses everywhere else. Return periods are a GEV fit to seasonal block maxima and are the softest number on the page - read them as an order of magnitude, not a figure.

Run statistics from each production's entry in aires/HANDOFF.md; figures from aires/amaps.py and aires/aplots.py on branch aires. Wave 1 - the four heat events and the persistence control - cost about 232 H100-hours across five runs and a gate; wave 2's five runs were budgeted at 61 to 64 H100-hours each.

A note on the two names without one: the p90_* cases are days selected by a national-mean anomaly threshold and carry no meteorological name, so they are named here by what they are - a nationwide warm spell at the 99th percentile, a normal-day test at the ensemble median, and a below-median day.
