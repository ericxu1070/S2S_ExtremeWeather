AI+RES · branch aires · adapter test

Reading the adapter figures

What each panel actually contains, what the 24-member mean is a mean over, why the two forecast means differ, and what the ten-case experiment concluded.

Q1

The 24-member mean is a mean over FCN3’s internal noise — not over perturbed initial conditions

There is exactly one initial condition per panel. The IC perturbation passed to earth2studio is literally Zero() in both fcn3/run_fcn3.py and aires/gate2.py. All 24 members start from the identical 72-channel field and diverge only because FourCastNet 3 is a stochastic model: its own noise generator is reseeded per shard with model.set_rng(seed = seed_for(event) + shard), 8 shards × 3 members.

So the two kinds of spread in these figures mean different things

  • Spread within one panel’s ensemble — purely FCN3 internal stochasticity. For the PNW dome, the per-member scatter of A_L is σ = 0.62 K.
  • Difference between the two panels — purely the initial condition, because member i on each side shares a noise stream with member i on the other. That pairing is verified rather than assumed: if the seeds had silently diverged, the paired difference would have equaled the ensemble’s own spread from the first step. It is 22% of it at day 1.5 and grows from there.

Q2

The variable is per case: nine cases are T2m anomaly, Hurricane Ian is 850 hPa wind speed

Each case declares one metric in fcn3/fevents.py, and every panel, colorbar and score in that case’s figure is in that metric’s own units. Nothing is averaged across metrics in physical units — the pooling in Q5 is done on a relative delta precisely so a wind case and a temperature case can share an axis.

Q3

The two means differ because of the adapter’s three derived channels — grown by 21 days of chaos

First, a labelling trap worth naming: the panels read “Native IC, 24-member mean” and “Adapter IC, 24-member mean”, but those are day-21 forecast fields, not initial conditions. The label names which IC the forecast came from.

Since the internal noise is matched member-for-member and the perturbation is Zero(), the entire difference between the two panels traces to the IC — and the IC differs in exactly three of its 72 channels:

Those residuals are tiny at hour 0, but a 21-day rollout sits far past the ~8.5 d predictability horizon Gate 2 measured for CONUS T2m. So the difference you see in the third panel is not the adapter’s error propagated linearly — it is chaos, triggered by the adapter’s error and then grown to saturation. That is why the difference field has weather-shaped structure (the PNW case peaks at 2.04 K, on a colorbar the caption itself flags as only 27% of the error scale) rather than a smooth bias pattern.

The curve that proves it — measured on T2m, the bit-identical channel

So the answer to “T2m was bit-identical, why does it look off?” is that FCN3 has no channel-wise isolation. The tcwv residual (~1 kg m⁻²) perturbs moisture → latent heating → geopotential → advection; the 100 m wind residual (0.63 m/s) perturbs boundary-layer fluxes. T2m inherits both on the first step, and then 84 steps of a chaotic system multiply it 12×.

By the verification week the two ensembles are independent draws

Paired-member rank correlation of A_L by lead: 0.99 at 0.25 d, 0.98 at 3 d, 0.95 at 8.5 d, 0.35 at 14 d, −0.01 at 21 d. Past ~8.5 d the pairing carries no information at all — which is exactly why the difference panels look like weather rather than a smooth offset, and why a per-case delta at 21 d lead is dominated by which member happened to land where.

The bound Gate 2 put on it

The paired IC-driven divergence never exceeds FCN3’s own internal noise at any lead: 22% of it at day 1.5, 51% at day 9, and only 0.990 at day-21 saturation. In other words, swapping a native IC for an adapter IC moves the forecast less than re-seeding the model does — at every single lead time.

Q4

“Observed” is truth, and the two error panels are ensemble-mean minus truth

Neither error panel is a single member, and nothing here is pooled across members before differencing. The map figure is a fixed 2×3 grid, and every cell is one of three things: truth, an ensemble mean, or a difference of those two.

Row 1 · left Observed (ERA5) The cached verification truth — a single field, no ensemble. Same 7-day-mean reduction as the forecasts, nearest-reindexed onto the cube grid with a 1e−4 tolerance so a float32 coordinate round-trip cannot silently produce an all-NaN difference. truth[metric], 1 field
Row 1 · centre Native IC, 24-member mean FCN3 from the ERA5-built IC. The day-21 forecast, averaged over all 24 members. Shares row 1’s colour scale with the other two so the three are directly comparable. native.mean(member)
Row 1 · right Adapter IC, 24-member mean FCN3 from the GenCast-state IC, same 24 seeds. This is the panel AI+RES actually depends on being right. adapter.mean(member)
Row 2 · left Native − observed The native ensemble mean’s forecast error field. The RMSE in its title is rmse_ens_mean: the cos(lat)-weighted RMS of this field — the error of the mean, not the mean of the members’ errors. native.mean(member) − truth
Row 2 · centre Adapter − observed Same reduction, adapter side. Rows 2 left and centre share one colour scale, so the honest comparison is how similar these two look — and they look nearly identical. adapter.mean(member) − truth
Row 2 · right Adapter − native The IC effect, isolated. Drawn on its own, much smaller scale — the caption states the ratio explicitly (27% of the error scale for the PNW dome) so the panel is never mistaken for being the same magnitude as the errors beside it. adapter.mean − native.mean

Two other reductions that appear in the numbers but not the maps

  • rmse_member_mean — the mean over members of each member’s own RMSE. Always larger than rmse_ens_mean (2.436 vs 1.640 K native, CONUS) because averaging cancels noise before scoring.
  • CRPS — the fair (unbiased) ensemble form, evaluated per grid point and then area-averaged. Not the CRPS of the area mean, which is a different and much smaller number.
  • A_L — the scalar the whole experiment sorts on: the cos(lat)-weighted area mean of the 7-day-mean field over the event box. One number per member; the panels are its un-reduced field.

Read the result as three separate statements, not one verdict:

  • Quote the bound, not the sign. The direction was never a coin flip a priori — the adapter IC carries real added error (0.63 m/s on the winds, 4.6% on tcwv) and adding IC error cannot help in expectation, so a small degradation is the prior, not a finding. What the experiment actually delivers is the upper bound: whatever the adapter costs at week 3, it is under ~5% of the native run’s RMSE.
  • The controls did their job. Four quiet days were added because all six events are exactly the flows where a small IC perturbation has the best chance of growing — an effect measured only on extremes cannot be told apart from one the extremes manufacture. The contrast came out at z = 0.67, so the six extremes were not a biased sample for this question. Adding the controls also moved the point estimate toward zero and tightened the detection limit by ~25%.
  • The per-case scatter is chaos, not adapter error. Individual cases run from −14.1% (p90_20231107) to +14.3% (the PNW dome) with standard errors of the same size, and I² is 0–15%. At 21 d lead, past the ~8.5 d predictability horizon, a paired difference is dominated by which member happened to land where.
Why this is the number that matters for AI+RES

The walker score is an ensemble mean. A bias this far under the ensemble’s own sampling noise — and under the 0.83 K by which ERA5 and HRRR disagree about what actually happened — cannot reorder walkers, which is the only thing the score is used for. The adapter is fit for its job.

Caveats that belong with the number

  • “No anomaly” means no CONUS-mean anomaly, not no weather. null_20240329 has a CONUS mean of +0.033 K over a strong regional dipole reaching about −8 K over the northern plains. Still the right control — a day nobody would have picked as an extreme, initialised and scored exactly like one — but the null group is not a quiescent atmosphere.
  • No summer control. Every summer non-event day in the frozen p90 control set predates the WeatherBench2 cutoff, so the controls are spring/autumn while two of the six events are warm-season. The contrast is not season-matched.
  • HRRR truth covers only three cases, so its CIs are correspondingly wide (+4.6% to +8.8%, all crossing zero). It leans the same way as ERA5.
  • More members would not have helped. Pushing the per-event s.e. below the effect needs M ≈ 100 on one event, and it would still be one draw of one atmosphere. Independent cases are what buy power: six unanimous cases bottom out at p = 0.031, ten reach p = 0.002.
How

Which script draws which figure

Every figure is drawn from cached cubes on CPU — no model runs. The maps and the numbers printed beside them come from one shared reduction (adaptest.verification_fields), so a figure cannot be drawn from a different reduction than its own caption.
