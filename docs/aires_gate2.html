What the gate is for

The score a walker receives must be a property of the walker, not of the adapter

In AI+RES a GenCast walker is stopped mid-rollout and its state is handed to FCN3 to be scored. The adapter is the joint. If passing a state through it perturbs the forecast more than FCN3's own stochasticity does, the resampling weights would be reacting to plumbing rather than to weather.

G2a answers this directly: on the longest score forecast the experiment ever runs, the ensemble mean is reproduced to 0.083 K. Every production score forecast is shorter and will agree better.

G2c answers it at every lead rather than one: the adapter's perturbation is strictly smaller than re-seeding the model throughout — 22% of the internal noise at day 1.5, 41% at day 7.5, 59% at day 10, and reaching 99% only at day 21 where both curves have saturated at the climatological spread.

The measurement

Three panels, one question each

The amendment

G2b could not be passed by anything, and the run proves it rather than argues it

G2b as originally specified in aires.md read paired-member rank correlation at the 21-day verification window. At that lead any difference in initial conditions — the adapter's, or simply re-seeding FCN3 — has been amplified to the internal-noise level and shuffles the members into an unrelated order. The measured 0.107 is what a perfect adapter would also score.

G2c is what turns that from an opinion into a measurement: at day 21 the ratio dpaired/dinternal is 0.990. By that lead the adapter's perturbation is a re-seed's, so a rank test there is measuring the atmosphere's Lyapunov exponent and reporting it as an adapter defect.

Resolving the same statistic by lead makes it well-posed. The two ensembles are member-for-member rank-equivalent out to 8.5 days; the decay past that is FCN3's predictability horizon for CONUS-mean T2m, not adapter error. G2b is now that horizon, and must reach at least 7 days.

The failure was never fatal, because G2b tests something AI+RES never reads. It is a member-identity test; the scoring path is walker state → adapter → FCN3 ensemble → mean → θ, which is exactly G2a's quantity. Member correspondence is never consumed at any lead.

Applied to aires.md and aires/gate2.py

G2b is now "ρs > 0.95 out to at least 7 d lead"; G2c is added as a required criterion; G2a is unchanged; and the M ≥ 24 requirement below is written into the spec. A finding the gate did not set out to make

The plan's M = 6 would have been unable to pass its own threshold

The run used 24 members instead of the planned 6, because the native-side ensemble was already cached from the FCN3-vs-GenCast experiment and cost nothing. That made the sampling noise floor measurable — and it is fatal to M = 6.

Cross-checks

Three things that could have been assumed and were instead measured

  1. Seed matching is verified by the data, not asserted. If adapter member i had drawn a different noise stream than native member i, dpaired would equal dinternal from the first step. It does not — it is 22% of it at day 1.5 and grows from there, which is only possible if the two members share an internal-noise stream and differ solely through the IC.
  2. The adapter is exact where it can be: 69 of the 72 FCN3 channels are bit-identical to the independently built native IC. The three derived channels come in at 0.634 / 0.609 m s−1 RMSE for u100m / v100m and 1.02 kg m−2 for tcwv — the same calibration that passed Gate 1.
  3. Gate 1 was rebuilt from scratch on a3mega and reproduced Derecho's numbers exactly (worst held-out 0.634 m/s u100m RMSE, 4.57% tcwv relative RMSE), so the calibration is reproducible across machines rather than a transferred artifact. The Gate 2 score stage has now been re-run on Derecho from the transferred cubes and reproduces every number on this page to the digit — Δmean −0.08344 K, ρ 0.10696, peak ratio 0.99003.

Cost and mechanics

What it took to run

Only the adapter side cost GPU time; the native ensemble was already on disk. Eight shards, three members each, on one 8×H100 node: ~11 min node wall, of which roughly 9 min was eight processes concurrently pulling the shared 4 GB model pickle off NFS with the GPUs idle. The rollout itself was 6.5 min at 1.3 s/step.

Short FCN3 jobs are load-bound, not compute-bound

The build-once model cache is still far cheaper than eight concurrent model builds, but at this job length the NFS read dominates. Worth remembering when sizing Gate 3's score forecasts, which are many short FCN3 runs rather than a few long ones.
